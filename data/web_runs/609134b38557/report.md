# Retention Analysis Report

**The reel lost over three-quarters of its audience due to jarring visual quality inconsistencies—specifically letterboxing and blurry close-ups—combined with a rapid, disconnected mid-video montage that felt like a generic photo slideshow.**

The reel retained 100% of viewers through the first two seconds before suffering an immediate 8.5 percentage point drop at t=3s due to heavy letterboxing abruptly transitioning to vertical video. Visual instability continued through t=4-6s with blurry close-ups and erratic framing pushing retention down to 75.8%. In the second half, rapid-fire cuts across scenic stills between t=10s and t=14s broke narrative momentum, slashing retention under 50% and ultimately tapering off to a 23.5% finish.

## Drop Events (ranked by impact)

### 1. t=2-3s -- 8.5 percentage points lost

**Why:** Severe letterboxing with prominent black borders in the opening footage created an unpolished look, driving immediate abandonment when abruptly cutting to vertical format.

**Evidence:** Retention dropped from 100.0% to 91.5% (z=3.04) at the cut on t=3s ('I have no'), matching a sharp -85% drop in sharpness and vision model notes identifying heavy black bars.

**Suggestion:** Crop and reframe the opening landscape footage to fill the 9:16 frame entirely from t=0s so viewers immediately see full-screen vertical content.

### 2. t=12-13s -- 6.5 percentage points lost

**Why:** A rapid succession of scenic photo cuts created fatigue, making the reel feel like an impersonal, generic slideshow rather than an engaging video story.

**Evidence:** Retention fell from 56.2% to 49.7% (z=2.0) across four consecutive cuts between t=10s and t=13s, accompanied by recurrent compression artifacts (z=2.0) and visual volatility.

**Suggestion:** Reduce the cut density from t=10-14s by replacing the 1-second landscape stills with a single 3-to-4 second continuous motion clip that directly illustrates the voiceover.

### 3. t=4-5s -- 5.9 percentage points lost

**Why:** A soft, out-of-focus close-up following rapid aspect ratio shifts disrupted visual clarity right as the speaker delivered the setup.

**Evidence:** Retention plummeted from 86.9% to 81.0% (z=1.69) at t=4s ('idea what I'm gonna do tomorrow') where the footage was flagged as blurry (z=-1.6) with severe sharpness instability (+2937% vs previous shot).

**Suggestion:** Remove or replace the blurry close-up clip at t=4s with sharp, in-focus subject footage that matches the vertical framing of the surrounding shots.

## Overall Suggestions

- Enforce strict 9:16 vertical framing across all timeline assets to eliminate black letterbox bars and erratic aspect ratio jumping.
- Smooth out the editing cadence by holding shots for at least 2 to 3 seconds during narrative exposition instead of cutting every 1 second.
- Standardize video export bitrate and resolve source file encoding issues to fix recurrent compression artifacts and softness flags (t=0-1s, t=10s, t=12s, t=17-18s).
